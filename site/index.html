01 / GEOMETRY & DUAL MOTION

An orbit is not the same as a pitch.

The centre of each elliptical blade travels clockwise around the rotor. At the same time, its chord turns at half that angular speed in the fixed frame. This is not one rigid rotating shape.

03 / RECORD THE SOURCE

Two surfaces. One physical flow.

The acoustic recording surface changes what must be sampled. It does not introduce a second sound to add to the first.

On the moving blades

Record pressure and boundary motion on the three body contours. Moving-boundary thickness and loading enter the implemented 2D analogy. The normal direction, traction convention and pressure conversion matter.

On an imaginary contour

Record pressure and fluid velocity on a closed stationary contour outside the moving interfaces. Fluid crosses this contour freely: it is not a porous wall or a resistance.

Native recording-contour evidence is unavailable until a completed case is loaded.

What the analogy does—and does not—separate

Both routes use a 2D free-space Green function. Their estimates overlap and are alternatives, never summed. The difference between them is not an isolated quadrupole measurement. Omitted viscous traction or exterior volume sources must be recorded in the evidence, not assumed zero.

The thin numerical extrusion uses empty front/back boundaries. Its arbitrary depth must be removed exactly once when forming force per unit span or contour integrals. No finite-span caps or span correction are invented.

04 / FROM SOURCE TO RECEIVER

Read time, frequency and direction together.

The synthesized history combines the selected frequency components. The spectrum shows their pressure amplitudes as coherent-window bin RMS, not a power spectral density. Directivity compares receivers around the same source, over the same retained window.

Units: acoustic pressure and the displayed spectral amplitudes are Pa, not Pa/m; source loading is N/m. Sound levels use 1 µPa reference. Band-limited synthesis is window-conditioned, not a recovered full-band waveform or a causal arrival record.

The selected band is exploratory. Excluding startup does not establish stationarity or a validated acoustic bandwidth. Use the overlays to inspect this calculation, not to rank the accuracy or real-world noise of the models.

One direction is shown for both overlaid cases. This control changes only the displayed history and spectrum; all twelve traces remain in the complete downloads. Source loading and the sparse polar samples are unchanged.

Receiver histories, spectra and directivity are pending native source histories and verified acoustic reduction.

The recorded observer set in the completed study is twelve positions on a 1 m radius, from 0° to 330° in 30° steps. That distance alone does not establish the acoustic far field. Sparse polar markers are not a continuously measured pattern.
